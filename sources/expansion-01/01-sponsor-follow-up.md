# FX-SRC-01 — Synthetic sponsor follow-up

Version 1. Scenario date: 2026-10-20. **Entirely invented role-play correspondence; no message sent, interview conducted or decision obtained.** Authority and fixture-premise definitions: [pack guide](README.md). No FX rule overrides the user-confirmed booking baseline.

## S01 — Why another discovery round

Leon, fictional sponsor: “The automatic booking decision removes an inbox step, not the need to know what physically left the workshop. If the portal says confirmed and the desk still can't find the collection, we have moved the confusion rather than removed it.”

Maya, fictional coordinator: “I want the booking, the physical loan and the item condition to stay distinguishable. Someone can reserve an item and never collect it. Someone can return it damaged. Neither situation is another approval request.”

Author framing: the extension follows an individually lendable item from discovery through confirmed booking, collection, actual loan, receipt and possible condition review. No reservation expiry, missed-collection consequence or damage-to-availability mapping is chosen by this framing.

## S02 — Baseline read-back, not a new decision

Facilitator: “For this exercise we inherit the user's UD-01–UD-08: automatic confirmation for an available interval with verified active membership; no training checks; reservation separation of at least 30 minutes, exactly 30 included; own booking privacy; no booking if inactive, unverifiable or unavailable. We will not ask the characters to overrule those decisions.”

Leon: “Good. Please don't turn the old Approve button into another launch story. But don't describe automatic booking as a promise that collection can always proceed; checkout eligibility and physical condition have not been settled.”

This is an invented read-back of existing authority, not evidence that Leon actually reconfirmed anything. The unchanged [canonical booking story](../../artifacts/stories/KD-ST-001-book-available-equipment.md) remains the reference.

## S03 — Core outcomes and competing emphasis

Leon: “I would show a member finding the right item, making a booking and knowing what happened to their own loan. At the desk I want collection and return work visible. I also want to notice equipment that hasn't come back.”

Maya: “The awkward cases are not decorative extras. If we can record a return but not hold a damaged item for review, the workflow tells an incomplete story. I'd prioritize that ahead of a reminder email.”

Aisha, fictional membership administrator: “A member seeing their own dates is different from everyone seeing who has a tool. Staff contact access needs a lending reason. I want the information carried by a return note considered separately from the fact of return.”

Competing priorities remain proposals: Leon prefers a compact demonstration; Maya prefers complete exception handling; Aisha prefers explicit data minimization before broad staff views. There is no voted scope or agreed implementation sequence.

## S04 — Boundary and non-goals

In this fictional follow-up the facilitator keeps payments/fines, purchasing, multi-site transfers, recurring bookings and public registration outside scope. Suggestions about waitlists, walk-in loans, substitutes, due-date extensions and staff overrides are parked, not adopted. The member still cannot record their own handover or return. Portal status visibility is distinct from email or other notifications.

Leon: “I'd like a reminder eventually, but don't put ‘email sent’ in the launch demonstration because I mentioned it. I haven't supplied who gets it, when, or what to do if it doesn't arrive.”

## S05 — Success measures are still a discovery problem

Leon: “Could success mean fewer desk emails?”

Maya: “Or fewer mismatches between the booking and the item handed over. A quieter inbox alone could mean members gave up.”

Facilitator notes: these are competing measure candidates, not a baseline or target. No sample, denominator, actual transaction volume, time saving or performance SLA exists. FX-Q14 asks which outcome to measure and how to obtain an appropriate baseline. No collection of real operational data is authorized.
