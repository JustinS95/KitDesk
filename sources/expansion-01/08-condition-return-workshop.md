# FX-SRC-08 — Synthetic condition and return workshop

Version 1. Scenario date: 2026-10-27. **Invented role-play workshop and author-defined extension premises; no real inspection, authority delegation or safety assessment.** Fictional Maya, Leon, an unnamed condition reviewer and Aisha supply competing perspectives. [Authority and limits](README.md).

## C01 — Return receipt and condition are different facts

Maya: “If someone hands back a damaged drill, it has still come back. I want the actual loan returned, the damage recorded and the item set aside, not the member left appearing to possess it.”

Fictional reviewer: “A receipt note tells me what staff saw. It doesn't tell me who caused it or whether it can be safely lent again.”

**FX-R13:** in the extension's correct-item damaged-return path, staff record actual receipt against the outstanding loan and note the observed concern. The loan becomes returned, and a related operational condition case identifies the physical item, receipt context, observed concern and restriction pending review. Treat this as an item set aside for assessment, not a fault finding or monetary obligation. A repeated receipt does not create a duplicate return/case for that same receipt. A failed or uncertain interaction must not announce both receipt and case as completed without knowing the result (FX-R21).

The restriction is an extension operational concept, **not a completed condition-to-availability policy for KD-ST-001**. Its representation, whether/how it prevents future booking/collection, and treatment of already-confirmed reservations remain FX-Q03. Do not claim that an existing reservation was silently cancelled, that membership changed or that a fine was created. Privacy of detailed notes remains FX-Q10.

## C02 — Who reviews the condition case

Desk colleague: “If I recorded the damage, couldn't I clear it when it looks fine?”

Leon: “For the demonstration I'd rather have someone explicitly responsible for the assessment. I haven't told you that every coordinator is qualified.”

**FX-R14:** solely for extension exercises, the author defines a **designated condition reviewer** actor who can inspect open condition cases, see item/concern and relevant loan/receipt context, and record the assessment outcome in C03. This is a fictional task designation, not a production role name, qualification standard or assignment to an actual person. Desk coordinators may see a related case's operational status for their lending task but cannot clear it unless separately designated in the exercise. Member, desk and manager titles do not imply that designation. A member cannot read internal case assessments or clear a case, including for an item they returned.

Case browsing is limited to condition work, not a borrower history/directory. Contact details are not included merely because a reviewer can assess an item. FX-Q08/10 ask who actually has authority and what information they require. FX-C06 is thus not “resolved by Leon”: the author supplied an explicitly provisional exercise actor, not stakeholder permission.

## C03 — Review outcomes and what they do not do

Fictional reviewer: “I need to know which concern I'm assessing. If I can't clear it, keep that clear in the record rather than treating my opening the case as sign-off.”

**FX-R15:** the designated reviewer can select an open case for the physical item, record an assessment and either retain its restriction with a reason or clear that specific condition case with an assessment outcome. The result remains tied to the item/case, not an arbitrary loan selected by a changed reference. An ordinary coordinator's attempted clearance is refused without changing the case. A case already cleared is not cleared again through the same open-case interaction; show the known current state. Separate/new concerns are not automatically cleared together.

Clearing a case records an assessment outcome, not universal availability or completion of any required inspection interval. It does not reopen a returned loan, erase a recorded concern, guarantee safety, amend membership or automatically restore/cancel later reservations. Actual evidence required to clear, incorrect-clearance reversal and availability qualification stay FX-Q03/08. No inspection checklist, certification or repair process is invented.

## C04 — Member report before return

Maya: “The member's online report should help whoever receives the item. I still need to inspect what comes back. Don't turn their description into a confirmed damage record before we have it.”

**FX-R19:** staff undertaking loan/return work can see a related member problem report in that actual loan's context, identified as member-reported content. This does not close the loan, record physical receipt or establish assessed damage. In this fixture a report alone does not create the damaged-return condition case in C01; staff receipt/observation is a separate step. Whether a reported concern should trigger any pre-return restriction or urgent response remains FX-Q03/13. The report is not shown to other members and does not expand staff access to unrelated records.

The member's own report remains visible to its author under FX-R18. Staff assessments are not automatically exposed to that member; what member-facing explanation is required for disputed condition remains FX-Q09/10. Do not promise email acknowledgement, monitored response, safety guidance or a due-date pause.

## C05 — Unsettled exception prompts

Maya: “What if they return the case without the charger? Or the right model with the wrong item label? Or disagree with the casing note?”

These are FX-Q09, not accepted routine/damaged-return criteria. Proposed discovery: walk through correct complete receipt, partial-kit receipt, wrong item, disputed damage and an erroneous return entry separately, identifying who can correct which facts. No correction, reversal, liability, repair approval or parts-purchasing capability is granted here. The consistent supported core is physical receipt versus condition assessment, not pretending every physical exception is resolved.
